koebelt dash now runs your Python scripts, with any package you like, and lets AI assistants help you, with the operator always in charge.

## Automate

- **Two ways to automate a device.** Program becomes Automate: a page that presents Sequences (steps in order, no code, run on the device) and Scripts (Python) side by side, with a guide, *Sequence or script?*, that compares them. On desktops the sidebar lists both; a pulsing dot marks the one that runs.
- **Sequences, by name.** What was called a program is a sequence everywhere: scripts call `dev.run_sequence("Survey square")` (the `*_program` names still work), and assistants `run_sequence`.

## Scripts

- **Drive a device from Python.** Automate > Scripts holds your scripts: create one from a template, import a `.py` or a `.zip` with its files, start from eight examples, edit in the built-in editor, check it, and run it on any connected device, simulated ones included. The screen opens the script you had open.
- **Any Python package.** Scripts run in your own Python, so OpenCV, PyTorch and YOLO work: the camera arrives as NumPy images, newest first, and `dev.overlay()` draws what your model sees over the camera in dash. The first time, *Set up Python* prepares an environment with the `koebelt-dash-scripts` package. A script that imports something its Python lacks offers to install it.
- **An editor that knows your code.** Completion for the API, your own names, the device's sticks, telemetry and sequences, the libraries you import (`np.`, `cv2.`) and file paths; parameter hints as you type; details when you hover a name.
- **Check before it moves.** Check finds syntax errors, missing imports, names that are not defined (`pritn`) and calls the device does not have, each underlined on its line; Run checks first.
- **Pictures and charts in Live.** `dev.show("tracking", frame)` puts a picture in the Live view (a camera frame with what the script found drawn on it, a mask, a matplotlib figure), `dev.chart()` a line, bar or scatter chart, and `dev.plot()` several series. They are recorded, and Replay shows them at their time.
- **A console that reads at a glance.** Errors in red, warnings in amber, `info:` lines in blue, `debug:` lines in grey (or Python's `logging`).
- **Steps and sequences.** Scripts take off, fly or drive to points, land, move arms and run your sequences, each returning once done. `# requires: drone, gps, camera` limits a script to the devices that fit.
- **Triggers.** Fire a script's events from a button or a switch (also on Control, next to the sticks), a key, a controller button, a condition (`battery.pct < 25 for 3s`), a device event or a timer.
- **You stay in charge.** STOP ends every script. Moving the sticks takes over until you resume. Each script has limits you set (speed, geofence, ceiling; no arming or parameter writes unless allowed). If a script crashes, hangs or leaves its geofence, the device holds and the script waits for you. A script runs only with a device connected.
- **Phones and tablets** run scripts on a *script host*: any computer on the same network running `koebelt-dash-scripts host`, paired once with a code. The phone keeps the device link, the sticks and STOP.
- **Vision without hardware.** The cameras of simulated drones and rovers show a yellow ball and a person walking near home: the Colour tracker and Follow a person examples work on them as they are.
- **Errors that explain themselves:** what went wrong, the line of your script, why, how to fix it, and the name you meant (koebelt-dash-scripts ERRORS.md).

## Modes

- A device connects disarmed in HOLD (simulated ones too: the drone waits on the ground). Arming hands it to you in MANUAL. AUTO is dash's: only while a sequence or a script drives the device, and it goes back to HOLD when that ends. MAVLink vehicles keep their own modes.
- The mode control is MANUAL | HOLD | AUTO, with AUTO lit and naming what drives; choosing a mode or moving the sticks takes over. Live has it in its bar, and choosing MANUAL there opens Control.

## Proximity sensors

- Ultrasonic, infrared and time-of-flight sensors show on Live from above, a wedge each, coloured by how close; the *Obstacle close* alert warns while armed. Scripts read them with `dev.proximity()`, and conditions with `prox.front` or `prox.nearest`. Devices declare them with koebelt/link 1.1.0 (Arduino, Python, Rust, ROS 2); the simulated rover and drone have some.

## AI assistants

- Turn on Settings > AI assistants to let Claude, Cursor, Codex, OpenCode or another MCP client read the guides, look at your devices and recordings, write, check and test scripts on simulated devices, and use the app.
- On real hardware, every action an assistant takes waits for your approval on a card in dash. You can allow a kind of action for the session; arming and take-off always ask. STOP never asks, and safety settings stay out of reach.
- koebelt dash sends nothing to any AI service itself.

## Every screen size

- Small phones, phones held sideways, tablets upright, small windows and ultrawide monitors: nothing overflows or overlaps, also with large system text and several devices connected. A tablet held upright gets its own Live layout; in a short window the sidebar scrolls; Replay scrolls on a short screen.

## Also

- **Maps from Mapbox.** Dark, Outdoors and Satellite maps replace OpenStreetMap's servers. You can use your own Mapbox token in Settings > Map.
- New guides: Scripts, AI assistants, and Sequence or script?
- Settings on a wide screen lists its sections beside them: click one to jump there.
- The chase view stays behind the drone as it yaws.
- Tilt control no longer reports errors on a computer, where there is no accelerometer.
- `koebelt-dash-scripts` 1.3.0 is on PyPI: `pip install koebelt-dash-scripts` (dash's *Set up Python* installs it by itself).

## Updating

From 1.1.0 or later, use the Update button in the app. From 1.0.0, install this version by hand once.

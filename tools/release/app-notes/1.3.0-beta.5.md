**Beta.** A beta of koebelt dash 1.3: scripts and AI assistants. It is offered only to apps with Settings > Updates > Betas on; please report problems on GitHub.

koebelt dash now runs your Python scripts, with any package you like, and lets AI assistants help you, with the operator always in charge.

## New in beta.5

- **Modes, simpler.** A device connects disarmed in HOLD (simulated ones too: the drone waits on the ground). Arming hands it to you in MANUAL. AUTO is dash's: only while a sequence or a script drives the device, and it goes back to HOLD when that ends. The mode control is MANUAL | HOLD | AUTO, with AUTO lit and naming what drives; choosing a mode or moving the sticks takes over. Scripts no longer switch to MANUAL themselves: arm the device, and dash drives it in AUTO for them.
- **The mode on Live.** Live has the mode control in its bar (on phones, the badge at the top lights up in AUTO); choosing MANUAL there opens Control, where the sticks are.
- **Proximity sensors.** Ultrasonic, infrared and time-of-flight sensors show on Live from above, a wedge each, coloured by how close; the *Obstacle close* alert warns while armed. Scripts read them with `dev.proximity()`, and conditions with `prox.front` or `prox.nearest`. Devices declare them with koebelt/link 1.1.0 (Arduino, Python, Rust, ROS 2); the simulated rover and drone have some.
- **What runs shows.** A pulsing dot marks Sequences and Scripts (and Automate) while one runs, and Automate's cards say what.
- **A console that reads at a glance.** Errors in red, warnings in amber, `info:` lines in blue, `debug:` lines in grey (or Python's `logging`); tracebacks and the library's errors colour themselves.
- **Follow a person** turns towards the person (it turned away), and both vision examples say what they see and do in the console.
- The Scripts screen opens the script you had open; the examples fold away once you have scripts of your own.

## New in beta.4

- **Automate.** Program becomes Automate: a page that presents the two ways to automate a device side by side, Sequences (steps in order, no code, on the device) and Scripts (Python), with a guide, *Sequence or script?*, that compares them. On desktops the sidebar always lists both.
- **Sequences, by name.** What was called a program is a sequence everywhere: scripts call `dev.run_sequence("Survey square")` (the `*_program` names still work), and assistants `run_sequence`.
- **Pictures and charts in Live.** `dev.show("tracking", frame)` puts a picture in the Live view (a camera frame with what the script found drawn on it, a mask, a matplotlib figure), `dev.chart()` a line, bar or scatter chart, and `dev.plot()` takes several series. Each is a widget: on the wall at once, on the HUD in a free spot.
- **An editor that knows your code.** Completion for the API, your own names, the device's sticks, telemetry and sequences, the libraries you import (`np.`, `cv2.`) and file paths; parameter hints as you type; details when you hover a name, with the whole docstring of a library function.
- **Recorded, and replayed.** What scripts show is kept with the recording: Replay shows the pictures and charts at their time, next to the telemetry.
- **Vision without hardware.** The cameras of simulated drones and rovers show a yellow ball rolling near home and a person in red walking round it: the Colour tracker example follows the ball on a simulated rover as is.
- **Check, for real.** Check now finds what would stop a script before it moves anything: syntax, imports this Python lacks (with the `pip install` to run), names that are not defined (`pritn`), and calls the device does not have or with the wrong arguments. Each problem is underlined on its line, and Run checks first: with errors, it asks before starting.
- **Missing packages.** A script that imports something its Python lacks offers to install it, on its page and when you press Run.
- **What a script needs.** `# requires: drone, gps, camera` limits a script to the devices that fit: the others are marked in its device menu, and Run says what they lack.
- **No device, no run.** Run waits until a device is connected (or the one chosen for the script is).
- **Maps from Mapbox.** Dark, Outdoors and Satellite maps replace OpenStreetMap's servers. You can use your own Mapbox token in Settings > Map.
- Installing or updating koebelt-dash-scripts says how it went, and stays a few seconds.
- The scripts folder link opens the folder (it tried to open it in a text editor on macOS).

## Scripts

- **Drive a device from Python.** Automate > Scripts holds your scripts: create one from a template, import a `.py` or a `.zip` with its files, start from eight examples, edit in the built-in editor, check it, and run it on any connected device, simulated ones included.
- **Any Python package.** Scripts run in your own Python, so OpenCV, PyTorch and YOLO work: the camera arrives as NumPy images, newest first, and `dev.overlay()` draws what your model sees over the camera in dash. The first time, *Set up Python* prepares an environment with the `koebelt-dash-scripts` package.
- **Steps and sequences.** Scripts take off, fly or drive to points, land, move arms and run your sequences, each returning once done.
- **Triggers.** Fire a script's events from a button or a switch (also on Control, next to the sticks), a key, a controller button, a condition (`battery.pct < 25 for 3s`), a device event or a timer.
- **You stay in charge.** STOP ends every script. Moving the sticks takes over until you resume. Each script has limits you set (speed, geofence, ceiling; no arming or parameter writes unless allowed). If a script crashes, hangs or leaves its geofence, the device holds and the script waits for you.
- **Phones and tablets** run scripts on a *script host*: any computer on the same network running `koebelt-dash-scripts host`, paired once with a code. The phone keeps the device link, the sticks and STOP.
- **Errors that explain themselves:** what went wrong, the line of your script, why, how to fix it, and the name you meant (koebelt-dash-scripts ERRORS.md).

## AI assistants

- Turn on Settings > AI assistants to let Claude, Cursor, Codex, OpenCode or another MCP client read the guides, look at your devices and recordings, write, check and test scripts on simulated devices, and use the app.
- On real hardware, every action an assistant takes waits for your approval on a card in dash. You can allow a kind of action for the session; arming and take-off always ask. STOP never asks, and safety settings stay out of reach.
- koebelt dash sends nothing to any AI service itself.

## Also

- New guides: Scripts, AI assistants, and Sequence or script?
- Settings on a wide screen lists its sections beside them: click one to jump there.
- The script panel shows the koebelt-dash-scripts version in each Python and offers updates; choosing a Python checks it again.
- The chase view stays behind the drone as it yaws.
- `koebelt-dash-scripts` 1.3.0b5 is on PyPI: `pip install --pre koebelt-dash-scripts` (dash's *Set up Python* installs it by itself). `show()`, `chart()`, `run_sequence()` and `proximity()` need it.

## Updating

From 1.1.0 or later: turn on Settings > Updates > Betas, then use the Update button, or choose it in Settings > Updates > Versions. From 1.0.0, install this version by hand once. The final 1.3.0 will be offered to everyone when it is out.
